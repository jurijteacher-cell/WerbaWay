## 🧩 3. Grammar: Э́то … — "this is …"

<aside class="lp-callout" data-tone="purple"><span class="lp-callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">💡</span><div class="lp-callout-body">

In sentences like this, Russian has <b>no "is"</b> — and, like Polish, no "a" or "the".

<b>Э́то телефо́н.</b> (This is a phone.)   ·   <b>Что э́то?</b> (What is this?)   ·   <b>Э́то не чай.</b> (This is not tea.)

</div></aside>

### No "is", no "a"

| English | Polish | Russian |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This <b>is a</b> phone. | To <b>jest</b> telefon. | <b>Э́то телефо́н.</b> |
| This <b>is a</b> book. | To <b>jest</b> książka. | <b>Э́то кни́га.</b> |
| I <b>am a</b> student. | <b>Jestem</b> studentem. | <b>Я студе́нт.</b> |
| Yurii <b>is a</b> teacher. | Jurij <b>jest</b> nauczycielem. | <b>Ю́рий — учи́тель.</b> |

📌 When both sides are nouns, Russian writes a dash: <b>Ю́рий — учи́тель.</b> After <b>я</b>, <b>ты</b> and <b>э́то</b> — no dash: <b>Я студе́нт. Э́то кни́га.</b>

### What? Who?

| Russian | Say it | English | Polish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <b>Что э́то?</b> | shto E-ta? | What is this? | Co to jest? |
| <b>Кто э́то?</b> | kto E-ta? | Who is this? | Kto to jest? |

📌 <b>Что</b> is said <b>shto</b>, not <i>chto</i>.

### No — не

<b>Э́то не чай. Э́то ко́фе.</b> — This is not tea. It's coffee. · Polish <i>To nie herbata.</i>

<b>Да, э́то …</b> — Yes, it's … · <b>Нет, э́то не …</b> — No, it's not …

### A question is just your voice

<b>Э́то ко́фе.</b> ↘ — a statement<br><b>Э́то ко́фе?</b> ↗ — a question: the voice goes up on <b>ко́фе</b>.

No question word like Polish <i>czy</i>, no new word order.

## 🧩 4. Grammar: он, она́, оно́ (he, she, it) — and мой (my)

<aside class="lp-callout" data-tone="purple"><span class="lp-callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">💡</span><div class="lp-callout-body">

Every Russian noun is <b>он</b> (he), <b>она́</b> (she) or <b>оно́</b> (it) — a phone too. You know this from Polish: <i>ten telefon, ta książka, to jabłko</i>. The last letter tells you.

<b>он — мой</b> (he — my)   ·   <b>она́ — моя́</b> (she — my)   ·   <b>оно́ — моё</b> (it — my)

</div></aside>

### Look at the last letter

|   | <b>он</b> (on) — he | <b>она́</b> (a-NA) — she | <b>оно́</b> (a-NO) — it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Last letter | consonant, <b>-й</b> | <b>-а</b>, <b>-я</b> | <b>-о</b>, <b>-е</b> |
| Examples | телефо́н (phone), чай (tea), музе́й (museum) | кни́га (book), ма́ма (mum), пе́сня (song) | окно́ (window), молоко́ (milk), мо́ре (sea) |
| Polish | ten | ta | to |

### Where is it? — Вот он!

<b>Где телефо́н? — Вот он!</b> (Where is the phone? — Here it is!)<br><b>Где ма́ма? — Вот она́!</b> (Where is mum? — Here she is!)<br><b>Где молоко́? — Вот оно́!</b> (Where is the milk? — Here it is!)

In Russian a phone is "he": <b>Где мой телефо́н? — Вот он!</b> (Where is my phone? — Here it is! Literally: “Here he is!”)

### My and your

|   | <b>он</b> (he) | <b>она́</b> (she) | <b>оно́</b> (it) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | <b>мой</b> телефо́н (moy)<br>my phone | <b>моя́</b> кни́га (ma-YA)<br>my book | <b>моё</b> окно́ (ma-YO)<br>my window |
| your | <b>твой</b> телефо́н (tvoy)<br>your phone | <b>твоя́</b> кни́га (tva-YA)<br>your book | <b>твоё</b> окно́ (tva-YO)<br>your window |
| Polish | mój, twój | moja, twoja | moje, twoje |

📌 <b>твой</b> = <i>your</i> when you talk to one friend — like Polish <i>twój</i>.

### Three exceptions

- <b>па́па</b> (dad) → <b>он</b>: мой па́па (my dad). A man is always он, even with -а at the end.
- <b>ко́фе</b> → <b>он</b>: мой ко́фе (my coffee). In casual speech you will also hear it as оно́. Polish <i>kawa</i> is "she" — Russian is different.
- <b>-ь</b> at the end → он or она́: <b>день</b> (day) — он, <b>ночь</b> (night) — она́. Learn these words together with мой or моя́.

### One small rule: Мо́жно + a "she" word

| <b>он / оно́</b> — no change | <b>она́</b> — <b>-а → -у</b> (and -я → -ю) |
| --- | --- |
| Мо́жно ко́фе? · Мо́жно сок? · Мо́жно моро́женое?<br>(Can I have coffee? · juice? · ice cream?) | вода́ → Мо́жно <b>во́ду</b>?<br>(Can I have water?) |

Polish does the same: <i>woda → Poproszę wodę.</i> 📌 вода́ → <b>во́ду</b>: the stress moves to the first syllable.
