## 3. The good news about Polish

### Fact 1 — Polish spelling is honest

English spelling lies to you. <i>Through, though, tough, thought, thorough</i> — six spellings, six different sounds, no rule.

Polish never does this. <b>One letter = one sound. Always. No exceptions. No silent letters.</b>

Once you learn &#126;15 symbols today, you can read <b>any</b> Polish word correctly — a street sign, a menu, a name you have never seen. You will not understand it yet. But you will pronounce it right. Most learners of English never get that, even after 10 years.

### Fact 2 — you already know about 100 Polish words

English and Polish borrowed from the same Latin and Greek pool.

<b>Take these as they are:</b>

telefon · komputer · internet · hotel · bank · park · problem · student · doktor · autobus · taksówka · pizza · hamburger · weekend · sport · film · muzyka · radio · adres · numer · lampa · teatr · biznes · menedżer · restauracja · apteka

<b>And one rule that unlocks a hundred more.</b> English <b>-tion</b> becomes Polish <b>-cja</b> (say: <i>-tsya</i>):

| English | Polish |
| --- | --- |
| information | informacja |
| station | stacja |
| situation | sytuacja |
| police | policja |
| tradition | tradycja |
| revolution | rewolucja |
| operation | operacja |
| organisation | organizacja |

So your Polish vocabulary on day one is not zero. It is about a hundred.

## 4. The alphabet — 4 blocks

Polish has 32 letters. Do not learn 32 things. Learn 4 groups.

### Block 1 — free letters (you already know these)

<b>a  b  d  e  f  g  k  l  m  n  o  p  s  t  z</b>

Same as English. Two small notes:

- <b>g</b> is always hard, as in <i>go</i>. Never as in <i>gentle</i>.
- <b>e</b> is always as in <i>bed</i>. Never as in <i>see</i>.

Try: <b>dom</b> (house) · <b>kot</b> (cat) · <b>bank</b> · <b>most</b> (bridge) · <b>lampa</b>

### Block 2 — false friends (look English, sound different)

These five are where beginners fail. Learn them today and half the battle is over.

| Letter | Sounds like | Example | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <b>w</b> | <b>v</b> | <b>w</b>oda (water) | VOH-dah |
| <b>j</b> | <b>y</b> | <b>j</b>a (I) | yah |
| <b>c</b> | <b>ts</b> in <i>cats</i> | <b>c</b>ena (price) | TSEH-nah |
| <b>y</b> | <b>i</b> in <i>bit</i> | t<b>y</b> (you) | ti |
| <b>u</b> and <b>ó</b> | <b>oo</b> in <i>food</i> | <b>u</b>lica / g<b>ó</b>ra | oo-LEE-tsah / GOO-rah |

<b>u</b> and <b>ó</b> are the same sound. Two spellings, one sound — history, nothing else.

<b>r</b> is rolled, like Spanish or Scottish. If you cannot roll it, an English <i>r</i> is fine for now. Nobody will misunderstand you.

Try: <b>Warszawa</b> (var-SHAH-vah) · <b>jutro</b> (YOO-troh, tomorrow) · <b>wino</b> (VEE-noh) · <b>syn</b> (sin, son)

### Block 3 — the Polish-only letters

Nine letters with tails, dots and strokes. This is the part that looks scary in photos and takes 15 minutes in real life.

| Letter | Sound | Example | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <b>ł</b> | <b>w</b> in <i>water</i> | <b>Ł</b>ódź | wooch |
| <b>ó</b> | <b>oo</b> | m<b>ó</b>j (my) | mooy |
| <b>ą</b> | <b>on</b> in French <i>bon</i> | m<b>ą</b>ż (husband) | monsh |
| <b>ę</b> | <b>en</b>, nasal | r<b>ę</b>ka (hand) | REN-kah |
| <b>ż</b> | <b>s</b> in <i>pleasure</i> | <b>ż</b>ona (wife) | ZHOH-nah |
| <b>ź</b> | same, but soft | <b>ź</b>le (badly) | zhleh |
| <b>ś</b> | <b>sh</b>, soft and hissy | <b>ś</b>roda (Wednesday) | SHROH-dah |
| <b>ć</b> | <b>ch</b>, soft | <b>ć</b>ma (moth) | chmah |
| <b>ń</b> | <b>ny</b> in <i>canyon</i> | ko<b>ń</b> (horse) | kony |

<b>ł = w</b> is the single biggest surprise. <i>Łódź</i> is not "lodz". It is <b>wooch</b>.

### Block 4 — two letters, one sound

| Pair | Sound | Example | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <b>sz</b> | <b>sh</b> in <i>ship</i> | <b>sz</b>afa (wardrobe) | SHAH-fah |
| <b>cz</b> | <b>ch</b> in <i>church</i> | <b>cz</b>as (time) | chahs |
| <b>rz</b> | <b>s</b> in <i>pleasure</i> (= <b>ż</b>) | <b>rz</b>eka (river) | ZHEH-kah |
| <b>ch</b> | <b>h</b>, breathy | <b>ch</b>leb (bread) | hlep |
| <b>dz</b> | <b>ds</b> in <i>kids</i> | bar<b>dz</b>o (very) | BAR-dzoh |
| <b>dż</b> | <b>j</b> in <i>jam</i> | <b>dż</b>em (jam) | jem |
| <b>dź</b> | soft <b>j</b> | <b>dź</b>więk (sound) | jvyenk |

<b>rz</b> and <b>ż</b> sound identical. <b>u</b> and <b>ó</b> sound identical. Poles argue about the spelling in school too — you are in good company.

## 5. Five reading rules

1. <b>One letter = one sound.</b> Read every letter you see. Nothing is silent.
1. <b>Stress is on the second-to-last syllable.</b> Always. <i>war-SZA-wa · her-BA-ta · kom-pu-TE-ro-wy</i> — no, wait: <i>kom-pu-te-RO-wy</i>. Count from the end, not the start.
1. <b>The letter i after a consonant softens it</b> and is not a separate vowel. <i>nie</i> = nyeh. <i>siedem</i> = SHYEH-dem. <i>ciasto</i> = CHAH-stoh.
1. <b>w = v · j = y · c = ts · ł = w.</b> If you remember only one line from today, remember this one.
1. <b>At the end of a word, voiced becomes voiceless.</b> <i>b→p, d→t, g→k, w→f, z→s.</i> So <i>Kraków</i> = KRAH-<b>koof</b>, <i>chleb</i> = hle<b>p</b>, <i>Łódź</i> = woo<b>ch</b>.

## 7. Survival phrases

### Hello and goodbye

| Polish | Say it | English | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cześć! | cheshch | Hi! / Bye! | friends, anyone your age |
| Dzień dobry! | jen DOH-bry | Good morning / afternoon | shops, offices, strangers, me |
| Dobry wieczór! | DOH-bry VYEH-choor | Good evening | after &#126;6 p.m. |
| Do widzenia! | doh vee-DZEH-nyah | Goodbye | formal |
| Na razie! | nah RAH-zhyeh | See you! | casual |
| Dobranoc! | doh-BRAH-nots | Good night | going to sleep |

### The five words that carry you through a country

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tak / Nie | tahk / nyeh | Yes / No |
| Proszę | PROH-sheh | Please / Here you go / You're welcome |
| Dziękuję | jen-KOO-yeh | Thank you |
| Przepraszam | psheh-PRAH-shahm | Sorry / Excuse me |
| Nie ma za co | nyeh mah ZAH tsoh | No problem |

<b>Proszę</b> is the most useful word in Polish. It means <i>please</i>, <i>here you are</i>, <i>you're welcome</i>, <i>go ahead</i>, <i>come in</i>, and <i>pardon?</i> depending on the tone. One word, six jobs.

### How are you

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jak się masz? | yahk sheh mahsh | How are you? |
| Dobrze, dziękuję. | DOH-bzheh, jen-KOO-yeh | Good, thank you. |
| A ty? | ah ti | And you? |
| Wszystko dobrze. | FSHIST-koh DOH-bzheh | All good. |
| Tak sobie. | tahk SOH-byeh | So-so. |

### When you are lost (use these constantly)

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nie rozumiem. | nyeh ro-ZOO-myem | I don't understand. |
| Nie wiem. | nyeh vyem | I don't know. |
| Powtórz, proszę. | POF-toosh, PROH-sheh | Repeat, please. |
| Wolniej, proszę. | VOL-nyey, PROH-sheh | Slower, please. |
| Co to znaczy? | tsoh toh ZNAH-chi | What does that mean? |
| Jak to jest po polsku? | yahk toh yest poh POL-skoo | How do you say that in Polish? |
| Mówisz po angielsku? | MOO-vish poh an-GYEL-skoo | Do you speak English? |

Say <b>Nie rozumiem</b> and <b>Powtórz, proszę</b> to me as often as you like. Both are correct Polish and both keep the lesson in Polish instead of in English.

## 12. How our lessons work

If we continue, this is the shape of a normal lesson. Nothing here is a lecture.

<b>You talk more than I do.</b> From minute one. Even at A0, with five words. That is the whole method.

<b>Words come in themed sets, not random lists.</b> 35–40 words per lesson, grouped — <i>at the doctor's</i>, <i>at the station</i>, <i>complaining about a delivery</i> — with flip cards you keep.

<b>Grammar is never a wall of tables.</b> It comes as small numbered cards, one idea per card, plus a card of the specific mistakes English speakers make. You get the thing native courses skip: not what Polish does, but where <i>your</i> language will push you the wrong way.

<b>We describe photos.</b> Two pictures side by side, you tell me the difference. This is the fastest way to force real speech out of a beginner.

<b>We role-play real situations.</b> Phone the internet provider. Return a broken charger. Book a table. You leave the lesson able to survive that exact situation.

<b>Homework is small and real.</b> A short video, one article, a few exercises, six lines of writing. Twenty minutes, not two hours.

<b>Everything lives in your digital notebook.</b> Every lesson, every word list, every exercise stays available. You never lose a file.

<b>Frequency:</b> twice a week works best at the start. Once a week works. Once a month does not.
