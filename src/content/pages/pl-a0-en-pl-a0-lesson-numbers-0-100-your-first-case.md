## 3. Numbers 0–10

| # | Polish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | zero | ZEH-ro |
| 1 | jeden | YEH-den |
| 2 | dwa | dvah |
| 3 | trzy | tshi |
| 4 | cztery | CHTEH-ri |
| 5 | pięć | pyench |
| 6 | sześć | sheshch |
| 7 | siedem | SHEH-dem |
| 8 | osiem | OH-shem |
| 9 | dziewięć | JEH-vyench |
| 10 | dziesięć | JEH-shench |

<b>Three sounds to watch:</b> <b>trzy</b> — after <i>t</i>, <i>rz</i> sounds like <i>sh</i>: <i>tshi</i>, not <i>t-r-zy</i>. <b>ę</b> before <b>ć</b> sounds like <i>en</i>: pięć = <i>pyench</i>. <b>ść</b> in sześć is two soft sounds together: <i>sh</i> + <i>ch</i>.

## 4. Numbers 11–20: -naście = -teen

| # | Polish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | jedenaście | yeh-deh-NAHSH-cheh |
| 12 | dwanaście | dvah-NAHSH-cheh |
| 13 | trzynaście | tshi-NAHSH-cheh |
| 14 | <b>czter</b>naście | chter-NAHSH-cheh |
| 15 | <b>pięt</b>naście | pyent-NAHSH-cheh |
| 16 | <b>szes</b>naście | shes-NAHSH-cheh |
| 17 | siedemnaście | sheh-dem-NAHSH-cheh |
| 18 | osiemnaście | oh-shem-NAHSH-cheh |
| 19 | <b>dziewięt</b>naście | jeh-vyent-NAHSH-cheh |
| 20 | dwadzieścia | dvah-JESH-chah |

<b>The pattern:</b> number + <b>-naście</b>, just like English number + <i>-teen</i>. Four numbers change a little (in bold): cztery → <b>czter</b>naście · pięć → <b>pięt</b>naście · sześć → <b>szes</b>naście · dziewięć → <b>dziewięt</b>naście. The strong syllable is always <b>NA</b>.

A teenager in Polish is <b>nastolatek</b> — someone in their <i>-naście</i> years.

</div></aside>

## 5. Tens: 20–100

| # | Polish | Say it | Ending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | dwa<b>dzieścia</b> | dvah-JESH-chah | 2 → -dzieścia |
| 30 | trzy<b>dzieści</b> | tshi-JESH-chee | 3, 4 → -dzieści |
| 40 | czter<b>dzieści</b> | chter-JESH-chee |   |
| 50 | pięć<b>dziesiąt</b> | pyen-JEH-shont | 5–9 → -dziesiąt |
| 60 | sześć<b>dziesiąt</b> | shesh-JEH-shont |   |
| 70 | siedem<b>dziesiąt</b> | sheh-dem-JEH-shont |   |
| 80 | osiem<b>dziesiąt</b> | oh-shem-JEH-shont |   |
| 90 | dziewięć<b>dziesiąt</b> | jeh-vyen-JEH-shont |   |
| 100 | sto | stoh | new word |

Three endings, three groups: <b>2</b> · <b>3–4</b> · <b>5–9</b>. Polish often treats 2, 3, 4 differently from 5 and up. You will see this again in section 7.

<b>Careful — just like </b><i><b>fifteen</b></i><b> and </b><i><b>fifty</b></i><b> in English:</b>

| -teen | -ty |
| --- | --- |
| 13 trzynaście | 30 trzydzieści |
| 14 czternaście | 40 czterdzieści |
| 15 piętnaście | 50 pięćdziesiąt |
| 16 szesnaście | 60 sześćdziesiąt |

<b>-naście</b> = <i>-teen</i> · <b>-dzieści / -dziesiąt</b> = <i>-ty</i>

## 7. Numbers in real life

### How old are you?

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ile masz lat? | EE-leh mahsh laht? | How old are you? <i>(literally: how many years do you have?)</i> |
| Mam trzydzieści lat. | mahm tshi-JESH-chee laht | I'm thirty. |
| Mam dwadzieścia dwa lata. | mahm dvah-JESH-chah dvah LAH-tah | I'm twenty-two. |

Polish uses <b>mam</b> (<i>I have</i>) for age, not <i>I am</i>.

### How much is it?

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ile to kosztuje? | EE-leh toh kosh-TOO-yeh? | How much is it? |
| Dwanaście złotych. | dvah-NAHSH-cheh ZWOH-tih | Twelve zlotys. |
| Trzy złote. | tshi ZWOH-teh | Three zlotys. |

### The 2-3-4 rule

<aside class="lp-callout" data-tone="blue"><span class="lp-callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">📏</span><div class="lp-callout-body">

<b>The number ends in 2, 3 or 4</b> → <b>lata</b> / <b>złote</b><br><i>Mam dwadzieścia trzy lata. · Cztery złote.</i><br><br><b>Everything else</b> → <b>lat</b> / <b>złotych</b><br><i>Mam dwadzieścia lat. · Mam czterdzieści pięć lat. · Dziesięć złotych.</i><br><br><b>12, 13, 14</b> are <i>-teen</i> numbers → <b>lat</b> / <b>złotych</b> · <b>21, 31, 41…</b> → <b>lat</b> / <b>złotych</b> too.<br><b>Just 1:</b> jeden złoty.

</div></aside>

| Polish | Say it | English |

## 8. Six phrases Poles really use

| Polish | Say it | Literally | Means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <b>Sto lat!</b> | stoh laht | A hundred years! | Happy birthday! — also the name of the Polish birthday song |
| <b>Na sto procent!</b> | nah stoh PROH-tsent | At a hundred percent! | Definitely! Absolutely sure! |
| <b>Raz, dwa, trzy!</b> | rahs, dvah, tshi | Once, two, three! | One, two, three! — when Poles count out loud, they start with <b>raz</b>, not <i>jeden</i> |
| <b>Dziękuję stokrotnie!</b> | jen-KOO-yeh stoh-KROT-nyeh | Thank you a hundred times! | Thanks a lot! (<i>stokrotka</i> = a daisy) |
| <b>Za pięć dwunasta</b> | zah pyench dvoo-NAHS-tah | Five to twelve | At the last minute |
| <b>Mieć dwie lewe ręce</b> | myech dvyeh LEH-veh REN-tseh | To have two left hands | To be clumsy. English has two left <i>feet</i> — Polish has two left <i>hands</i> |

<b>dwie</b> is <i>two</i> for feminine words like <i>ręka</i> (hand). <b>dwa</b> is for masculine words. More about this later.

## 9. What is a case?

### English has cases too

<aside class="lp-callout" data-tone="gray"><span class="lp-callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">🇬🇧</span><div class="lp-callout-body">

<b>I</b> see <b>him</b>. — <b>He</b> sees <b>me</b>.<br>I / me · he / him · she / her · who / whom<br>Same person, different form — because the <b>job</b> of the word in the sentence is different. That is a case.

</div></aside>

English does this only with a few small words. <b>Polish does it with almost every word</b>: names, nouns, adjectives. The <b>ending</b> tells you the job of the word.

### Why is this useful?

In English, <b>word order</b> shows who does what. <i>The dog bites the man</i> ≠ <i>The man bites the dog</i>.

In Polish, the <b>ending</b> shows it:

| Polish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Tomek kocha <b>Anię</b>. | Tomek loves Ania. |
| <b>Anię</b> kocha Tomek. | Tomek loves Ania. <i>(same meaning — different emphasis)</i> |
| <b>Ania</b> kocha Tomka. | Ania loves Tomek. |

<b>Anię</b> = she is loved. <b>Ania</b> = she loves. The ending did the job, not the order.

### Seven cases — one word, seven faces

| # | Question | Form | Example | English | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | kto? co? | mam<b>a</b> | To jest mama. | This is mum. | the subject, the dictionary form |
| 2 | kogo? czego? | mam<b>y</b> | Nie ma mamy. | Mum isn't here. | "of", "no …" |
| 3 | komu? czemu? | mam<b>ie</b> | Dziękuję mamie. | I thank mum. | "to / for someone" |
| 4 | kogo? co? | mam<b>ę</b> | Kocham mamę. | I love mum. | the object |
| <b>5</b> | <b>kim? czym?</b> | <b>mamą</b> | <b>Mieszkam z mamą.</b> | <b>I live with mum.</b> | <b>"with", "as", "by" — today!</b> |
| 6 | o kim? o czym? | mam<b>ie</b> | Myślę o mamie. | I think about mum. | "about", "in", "on" |
| 7 | — | mam<b>o</b> | Mamo! | Mum! | calling someone |

<b>You don't learn this table.</b> We take the cases one by one, always with real sentences. Today: <b>row 5 — narzędnik</b>.

</div></aside>

## 10. Narzędnik — the "with / as / by" case

<b>Narzędnik</b> (<i>nah-ZHEND-neek</i>) comes from <b>narzędzie</b> — <i>a tool</i>. Questions: <b>kim?</b> (who?) · <b>czym?</b> (what?)

### The endings

| The word is… | Ending | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <b>he</b> (ends in a consonant) or <b>it</b> (ends in -o) | <b>-em</b> | nauczyciel → nauczyciel<b>em</b> · brat → brat<b>em</b> · autobus → autobus<b>em</b> |
| ends in <b>-k, -g</b> (and -ko, -go) | <b>-iem</b> | Anglik → Anglik<b>iem</b> · mleko → mlek<b>iem</b> · pociąg → pociąg<b>iem</b> |
| <b>she</b> (ends in -a) | <b>-ą</b> | mama → mam<b>ą</b> · siostra → siostr<b>ą</b> · taksówka → taksówk<b>ą</b> |

<b>ą</b> at the end of a word sounds like <b>om</b>: mamą = <i>MAH-mom</i> · taksówką = <i>tahk-SOOF-kom</i>.

### Job 1 · Who are you? — jestem + narzędnik

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kim jesteś? | keem YES-tesh? | Who are you? / What do you do? |
| Jestem Anglikiem. | YES-tem ahn-GLEE-kyem | I'm English. <i>(a man)</i> |
| Jestem Angielką. | YES-tem ahn-GYEL-kom | I'm English. <i>(a woman)</i> |
| Jestem nauczycielem. | YES-tem now-chi-CHEH-lem | I'm a teacher. |
| Jestem studentem. | YES-tem stoo-DEN-tem | I'm a student. |

<aside class="lp-callout" data-tone="red"><span class="lp-callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">⚠️</span><div class="lp-callout-body">

<b>"To jest" does not change the word.</b><br><b>On jest</b> nauczyciel<b>em</b>. — He is a teacher. <i>(who he is → narzędnik)</i><br><b>To jest</b> nauczyciel. — This is a teacher. <i>(pointing → dictionary form)</i>

</div></aside>

### Job 2 · With whom? With what? — z + narzędnik

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Z kim mieszkasz? | s keem MYESH-kahsh? | Who do you live with? |
| Mieszkam z bratem. | MYESH-kahm z BRAH-tem | I live with my brother. |
| Rozmawiam z mamą. | roz-MAH-vyahm z MAH-mom | I'm talking to mum. |
| Kawa z mlekiem. | KAH-vah z MLEH-kyem | Coffee with milk. |
| Kawa z cukrem. | KAH-vah s TSOO-krem | Coffee with sugar. |

### Job 3 · A tool or transport — narzędnik without z

| Polish | Say it | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Czym jeździsz? | chim YEZH-jeesh? | How do you travel? <i>(literally: by what?)</i> |
| Jeżdżę autobusem. | YEZH-jeh ow-toh-BOO-sem | I go by bus. |
| Jeżdżę pociągiem. | YEZH-jeh poh-CHON-gyem | I go by train. |
| Jeżdżę samochodem. | YEZH-jeh sah-moh-HOH-dem | I go by car. |
| Jeżdżę taksówką. | YEZH-jeh tahk-SOOF-kom | I go by taxi. |
| Piszę długopisem. | PEE-sheh dwoo-goh-PEE-sem | I write with a pen. |

<aside class="lp-callout" data-tone="red"><span class="lp-callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">⚠️</span><div class="lp-callout-body">

<b>English says </b><i><b>with</b></i><b> and </b><i><b>by</b></i><b> — Polish says z only for "together with".</b><br>✅ Mieszkam <b>z</b> bratem. — together with my brother<br>✅ Jeżdżę autobusem. — the bus is the transport<br>❌ Jeżdżę <b>z</b> autobusem. — sounds like the bus is your travel friend

</div></aside>

### Small exceptions

- Men's words that end in <b>-a</b> also take <b>-ą</b>: programista → programist<b>ą</b> · kierowca (driver) → kierowc<b>ą</b>
- Some words change a little inside: sam<b>ó</b>chód → samoch<b>o</b>dem · Ukrainiec → Ukraińcem
- <b>ze mną</b> = with me
- Bike: <b>Jeżdżę rowerem</b> and <b>Jeżdżę na rowerze</b> — both correct
- On foot: <b>pieszo</b> — no case
