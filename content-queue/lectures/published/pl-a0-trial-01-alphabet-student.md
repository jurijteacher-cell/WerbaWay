## 3. The good news about Polish

### Fact 1 — Polish spelling is honest

English spelling lies to you. *Through, though, tough, thought, thorough* — six spellings, six different sounds, no rule.

Polish never does this. **One letter = one sound. Always. No exceptions. No silent letters.**

Once you learn ~15 symbols today, you can read **any** Polish word correctly — a street sign, a menu, a name you have never seen. You will not understand it yet. But you will pronounce it right. Most learners of English never get that, even after 10 years.

### Fact 2 — you already know about 100 Polish words

English and Polish borrowed from the same Latin and Greek pool.

**Take these as they are:**

telefon · komputer · internet · hotel · bank · park · problem · student · doktor · autobus · taksówka · pizza · hamburger · weekend · sport · film · muzyka · radio · adres · numer · lampa · teatr · biznes · menedżer · restauracja · apteka

**And one rule that unlocks a hundred more.** English **-tion** becomes Polish **-cja** (say: *-tsya*):

| English | Polish |
|---|---|
| information | informacja |
| station | stacja |
| situation | sytuacja |
| police | policja |
| tradition | tradycja |
| revolution | rewolucja |
| operation | operacja |
| organisation | organizacja |

So your Polish vocabulary on day one is not zero. It is about a hundred.

## 4. The alphabet — 4 blocks

Polish has 32 letters. Do not learn 32 things. Learn 4 groups.

### Block 1 — free letters (you already know these)

**a  b  d  e  f  g  k  l  m  n  o  p  s  t  z**

Same as English. Two small notes:

- **g** is always hard, as in *go*. Never as in *gentle*.
- **e** is always as in *bed*. Never as in *see*.

Try: **dom** (house) · **kot** (cat) · **bank** · **most** (bridge) · **lampa**

### Block 2 — false friends (look English, sound different)

These five are where beginners fail. Learn them today and half the battle is over.

| Letter | Sounds like | Example | Say it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **w** | **v** | **w**oda (water) | VOH-dah |
| **j** | **y** | **j**a (I) | yah |
| **c** | **ts** in *cats* | **c**ena (price) | TSEH-nah |
| **y** | **i** in *bit* | t**y** (you) | ti |
| **u** and **ó** | **oo** in *food* | **u**lica / g**ó**ra | oo-LEE-tsah / GOO-rah |

**u** and **ó** are the same sound. Two spellings, one sound — history, nothing else.

**r** is rolled, like Spanish or Scottish. If you cannot roll it, an English *r* is fine for now. Nobody will misunderstand you.

Try: **Warszawa** (var-SHAH-vah) · **jutro** (YOO-troh, tomorrow) · **wino** (VEE-noh) · **syn** (sin, son)

### Block 3 — the Polish-only letters

Nine letters with tails, dots and strokes. This is the part that looks scary in photos and takes 15 minutes in real life.

| Letter | Sound | Example | Say it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ł** | **w** in *water* | **Ł**ódź | wooch |
| **ó** | **oo** | m**ó**j (my) | mooy |
| **ą** | **on** in French *bon* | m**ą**ż (husband) | monsh |
| **ę** | **en**, nasal | r**ę**ka (hand) | REN-kah |
| **ż** | **s** in *pleasure* | **ż**ona (wife) | ZHOH-nah |
| **ź** | same, but soft | **ź**le (badly) | zhleh |
| **ś** | **sh**, soft and hissy | **ś**roda (Wednesday) | SHROH-dah |
| **ć** | **ch**, soft | **ć**ma (moth) | chmah |
| **ń** | **ny** in *canyon* | ko**ń** (horse) | kony |

**ł = w** is the single biggest surprise. *Łódź* is not "lodz". It is **wooch**.

### Block 4 — two letters, one sound

| Pair | Sound | Example | Say it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sz** | **sh** in *ship* | **sz**afa (wardrobe) | SHAH-fah |
| **cz** | **ch** in *church* | **cz**as (time) | chahs |
| **rz** | **s** in *pleasure* (= **ż**) | **rz**eka (river) | ZHEH-kah |
| **ch** | **h**, breathy | **ch**leb (bread) | hlep |
| **dz** | **ds** in *kids* | bar**dz**o (very) | BAR-dzoh |
| **dż** | **j** in *jam* | **dż**em (jam) | jem |
| **dź** | soft **j** | **dź**więk (sound) | jvyenk |

**rz** and **ż** sound identical. **u** and **ó** sound identical. Poles argue about the spelling in school too — you are in good company.

## 5. Five reading rules

1. **One letter = one sound.** Read every letter you see. Nothing is silent.
2. **Stress is on the second-to-last syllable.** Always. *war-SZA-wa · her-BA-ta · kom-pu-TE-ro-wy* — no, wait: *kom-pu-te-RO-wy*. Count from the end, not the start.
3. **The letter i after a consonant softens it** and is not a separate vowel. *nie* = nyeh. *siedem* = SHYEH-dem. *ciasto* = CHAH-stoh.
4. **w = v · j = y · c = ts · ł = w.** If you remember only one line from today, remember this one.
5. **At the end of a word, voiced becomes voiceless.** *b→p, d→t, g→k, w→f, z→s.* So *Kraków* = KRAH-**koof**, *chleb* = hle**p**, *Łódź* = woo**ch**.

## 7. Survival phrases

### Hello and goodbye

| Polish | Say it | English | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cześć! | cheshch | Hi! / Bye! | friends, anyone your age |
| Dzień dobry! | jen DOH-bry | Good morning / afternoon | shops, offices, strangers, me |
| Dobry wieczór! | DOH-bry VYEH-choor | Good evening | after ~6 p.m. |
| Do widzenia! | doh vee-DZEH-nyah | Goodbye | formal |
| Na razie! | nah RAH-zhyeh | See you! | casual |
| Dobranoc! | doh-BRAH-nots | Good night | going to sleep |

### The five words that carry you through a country

| Polish | Say it | English |
|---|---|---|
| Tak / Nie | tahk / nyeh | Yes / No |
| Proszę | PROH-sheh | Please / Here you go / You're welcome |
| Dziękuję | jen-KOO-yeh | Thank you |
| Przepraszam | psheh-PRAH-shahm | Sorry / Excuse me |
| Nie ma za co | nyeh mah ZAH tsoh | No problem |

**Proszę** is the most useful word in Polish. It means *please*, *here you are*, *you're welcome*, *go ahead*, *come in*, and *pardon?* depending on the tone. One word, six jobs.

### How are you

| Polish | Say it | English |
|---|---|---|
| Jak się masz? | yahk sheh mahsh | How are you? |
| Dobrze, dziękuję. | DOH-bzheh, jen-KOO-yeh | Good, thank you. |
| A ty? | ah ti | And you? |
| Wszystko dobrze. | FSHIST-koh DOH-bzheh | All good. |
| Tak sobie. | tahk SOH-byeh | So-so. |

### When you are lost (use these constantly)

| Polish | Say it | English |
|---|---|---|
| Nie rozumiem. | nyeh ro-ZOO-myem | I don't understand. |
| Nie wiem. | nyeh vyem | I don't know. |
| Powtórz, proszę. | POF-toosh, PROH-sheh | Repeat, please. |
| Wolniej, proszę. | VOL-nyey, PROH-sheh | Slower, please. |
| Co to znaczy? | tsoh toh ZNAH-chi | What does that mean? |
| Jak to jest po polsku? | yahk toh yest poh POL-skoo | How do you say that in Polish? |
| Mówisz po angielsku? | MOO-vish poh an-GYEL-skoo | Do you speak English? |

Say **Nie rozumiem** and **Powtórz, proszę** to me as often as you like. Both are correct Polish and both keep the lesson in Polish instead of in English.

## 8. Now you — introduce yourself

Fill in the blanks. This is your homework sentence set and your first real conversation.

> **Cześć!** — *cheshch*
> **Mam na imię ________.** — *mahm nah EE-myeh …* (My name is …)
> **Jestem z Anglii.** — *YES-tem z AN-glee* (I'm from England.)
> **Mieszkam w ________.** — *MYESH-kahm v …* (I live in …)
> **Mam ________ lat.** — *mahm … laht* (I'm … years old.)
> **Uczę się polskiego.** — *OO-cheh sheh pol-SKYEH-goh* (I'm learning Polish.)
> **Miło mi!** — *MEE-woh mee* (Nice to meet you!)

### Numbers you need for your age

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 jeden | 2 dwa | 3 trzy | 4 cztery |
| 5 pięć | 6 sześć | 7 siedem | 8 osiem |
| 9 dziewięć | 10 dziesięć | 20 dwadzieścia | 30 trzydzieści |
| 40 czterdzieści | 50 pięćdziesiąt | 60 sześćdziesiąt | 70 siedemdziesiąt |

Build it like English: **dwadzieścia pięć** = twenty-five. **trzydzieści siedem** = thirty-seven.

One small rule: after **2, 3, 4** (and 22, 23, 24, 32, 33, 34…) say **lata**. After everything else say **lat**.
*Mam dwadzieścia dwa **lata**. Mam dwadzieścia pięć **lat**.*

## 12. How our lessons work

If we continue, this is the shape of a normal lesson. Nothing here is a lecture.

**You talk more than I do.** From minute one. Even at A0, with five words. That is the whole method.

**Words come in themed sets, not random lists.** 35–40 words per lesson, grouped — *at the doctor's*, *at the station*, *complaining about a delivery* — with flip cards you keep.

**Grammar is never a wall of tables.** It comes as small numbered cards, one idea per card, plus a card of the specific mistakes English speakers make. You get the thing native courses skip: not what Polish does, but where *your* language will push you the wrong way.

**We describe photos.** Two pictures side by side, you tell me the difference. This is the fastest way to force real speech out of a beginner.

**We role-play real situations.** Phone the internet provider. Return a broken charger. Book a table. You leave the lesson able to survive that exact situation.

**Homework is small and real.** A short video, one article, a few exercises, six lines of writing. Twenty minutes, not two hours.

**Everything lives in your digital notebook.** Every lesson, every word list, every exercise stays available. You never lose a file.

**Frequency:** twice a week works best at the start. Once a week works. Once a month does not.
