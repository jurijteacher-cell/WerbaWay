# Polish in One Hour: the Alphabet and Your First Conversation

> **A note on this file.** The body of this lesson is in English, not Polish. The student has zero Polish and no Ukrainian, so English is the only working language available. Every Polish item carries a pronunciation line in the third column. That column is not decoration — it is the whole lesson.

---

## 1. Czytanie

You will read this text at minute 48, not now. It is 115 words of real Polish, written for you: every sound you learn today appears in it at least twice.

### Dzień dobry! Jestem Jurij

Dzień dobry! Mam na imię Jurij. Jestem z Ukrainy, z miasta Iwano-Frankiwsk. To nie jest duże miasto, ale jest tu uniwersytet, teatr, muzeum, park i dobra restauracja.

Mam trzydzieści lat. Jestem lektorem: uczę polskiego. To moja praca i moja pasja. Siedem lat mieszkałem i studiowałem w Polsce, we Wrocławiu. Wrocław to piękne miasto: rzeka Odra, stary most, duży rynek.

![Wrocław Ostrów Tumski across the Odra at dusk](/content/pl-a0-trial-01-alfabet/inline-1.jpg)

Mam małe mieszkanie w mieście, ale mam też mały dom w górach. Lubię tam jeździć. Dom jest stary i cichy. Zimą jest śnieg. Rano jest kawa, chleb i dżem, a wieczorem tylko lampa, herbata i cisza.

Sport? Tak. Chodzę na trening: to boks tajski. Czasem gram w gry komputerowe — strategia, akcja, czasem online.

A ty? Jak masz na imię? Skąd jesteś? Gdzie mieszkasz? Dlaczego uczysz się polskiego?

Bardzo mi miło. Dziękuję i do zobaczenia!

## 2. Pytania do tekstu

Answer in English. You are being tested on decoding, not on speaking.

1. Find four words you understood before anyone translated them. Why did you understand them?
2. Where did Jurij study, and for how long? Which word in the text told you „seven"?
3. What is in the mountain house in the morning, and what is there in the evening?
4. The text ends with five questions. Which of them is about your reason for learning Polish?
5. Find every word in the text containing **ł**. How many are there, and did you pronounce all of them as **w**?

## 4. Praktyka mówienia

Seven questions. You answer in English; each answer ends with one Polish phrase from the board. The point is not fluency today — it is that you finish every thought with a Polish sound in your mouth.

- Why Polish, and why now? What happens in your life if you do nothing about it? *End with:* **Uczę się polskiego.**
- You have looked at written Polish before today. What exactly scared you — the length of the words, the letters, or the consonant clusters?
- Which is harder to learn and why: a language with difficult spelling and easy grammar, or one with easy spelling and difficult grammar?
- English has *through, though, tough, thought, thorough.* Is that a flaw in English, or the price of a language that absorbed everything it met?
- Would you rather be understood with a strong accent, or silent and correct? Be honest — your answer changes how I teach you.
- How much time can you actually give this every week? Not the ideal number. The real one.
- Six months from now, what is the one sentence in Polish you want to be able to say without thinking?

## 7. Gramatyka: the Polish sound system

[BOARD: id="polski-system-dzwiekow", title="How Polish reading works — the board", layout="cards", columns="3", style="sketch-board", note="każdy H3 = karta z numerem w kółku, wiersze z ✦ = kolorowe plakietki, akapit z 📌 = żółta karteczka na dole karty"]

### ① Why this is good news

English spelling is a museum. Polish spelling is a machine.

✦ through · though · tough · thought
✦ four spellings, four sounds, no rule
✦ Polish: one letter, one sound, no exceptions

📌 You do not memorise Polish words letter by letter. You learn 15 symbols and read everything.

### ② The formula

What you see is what you say.

✦ every letter is pronounced
✦ nothing is silent
✦ nothing changes with mood or position

📌 There is no schwa in Polish. Unstressed vowels stay fully themselves.

### ③ Five false friends

They look English and lie.

✦ w = v · woda, Warszawa
✦ j = y · jutro, ja
✦ c = ts · cena, noc
✦ y = i in *bit* · syn, ty
✦ ó = oo · góra, mój

📌 If you remember one card from today, this is the card.

### ④ Ł is W

The single biggest surprise in the alphabet.

✦ mały = MAH-wy
✦ Wisła = VEES-wah
✦ Łódź = wooch

📌 Never „L". English speakers who get this one right sound instantly better than tourists who have lived there a year.

### ⑤ The tails: ą, ę

Nasal vowels. Say them through the nose.

✦ ą ≈ French *bon* · mąż, wąż
✦ ę ≈ French *cousin* · ręka, pięć
✦ at the end of a word, ę relaxes: imię ≈ EE-myeh

📌 Nobody expects nasals to be perfect on day one. An honest *on* and *en* is already understood.

### ⑥ The strokes: ć, ś, ź, ń

Soft versions of sounds you already have.

✦ ć ≈ soft ch · być
✦ ś ≈ soft sh · środa
✦ ź ≈ soft zh · źle
✦ ń ≈ ny in *canyon* · koń

📌 Smile slightly while saying them. That is physically most of the softening.

### ⑦ The dot: ż

One letter, one very English sound.

✦ ż = s in *pleasure*
✦ żona, duży, może
✦ same sound as rz

📌 ż and rz are identical in speech. Polish children lose marks on this too.

### ⑧ Hard digraphs

Two letters walking as one.

✦ sz = sh · szafa
✦ cz = ch · czas
✦ rz = zh · rzeka
✦ ch = h, breathy · chleb

📌 sz cz rz next to each other is not cruelty. It is just three ordinary sounds in a row.

### ⑨ Voiced digraphs

The d-family.

✦ dz = ds in *kids* · bardzo
✦ dż = j in *jam* · dżem
✦ dź = soft j · dźwięk

📌 dzi before a vowel is the same as dź: dzień = jen.

### ⑩ The letter i softens

Here i is a tool, not a vowel.

✦ ni = ń · nie = nyeh
✦ si = ś · siedem = SHYEH-dem
✦ ci = ć · ciasto = CHAH-stoh

📌 So „nie" is one syllable, not two. This single point fixes half of all beginner mistakes.

### ⑪ Stress

Free of charge, forever.

✦ always the second-to-last syllable
✦ her-BA-ta · dzię-KU-ję · Szcze-BRZE-szyn
✦ count from the end, never from the start

📌 English stress has no rule and native speakers argue about it. Polish just hands you the answer.

### ⑫ Ends of words go quiet

The last consonant loses its voice.

✦ b → p · chleb = hlep
✦ d → t · lód = loot
✦ w → f · Kraków = KRAH-koof
✦ ź → ć · Łódź = wooch

📌 This is why Kraków does not rhyme with how it looks. It is a rule, not an exception.

### ⑬ Things Polish does not have

Shorter list than you feared.

✦ no q, v, x except in foreign words
✦ no silent letters
✦ no „magic e"
✦ no th sound at all

📌 You will never meet a Polish word whose pronunciation you cannot work out from the page.

### ⑭ Traps for English speakers

Six habits English will push on you.

✦ Wisła is VEES-wah, not WISS-lah
✦ cena is TSEH-nah, not SEH-nah
✦ Jan is yahn, not jan
✦ syn is sin, not seen
✦ nie is nyeh, one syllable
✦ Kraków is KRAH-koof, not kra-KOW

📌 Every one of these is your mouth doing English out of habit. Slow down by half and they disappear.

## 10. Praca domowa

Twenty minutes. Not two hours. Do it within 48 hours or it does nothing.

[EXERCISE: file="pl-a0-trial-01-alfabet-hw.json", type="fill-in-blank"]

### Wideo

Watch **„Polish Alphabet & Pronunciation"**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cOuTPA6ufXA

It goes letter by letter with a native voice. Do not just watch — pause and repeat after every letter.

Answer in writing (one or two sentences each):
1. Which three letters were hardest for your mouth?
2. Find one letter the video pronounces differently from how I did. Which one, and what is the difference?
3. Write down two words from the video that we did not use in the lesson.

### Do czytania

Read **„Polish Alphabet & Pronunciation"** on Mówić po polsku: https://mowicpopolsku.com/polish-alphabet-pronunciation/

Every letter has an audio button. There is also a free two-page PDF at the bottom of the page — download it and keep it on your phone.

Then write out these eight words, and next to each one write your own pronunciation guess: *żółty · Wisła · dziewczyna · chłopiec · śniadanie · kościół · wrzesień · szczęście.*

One warning: that page says **ź** sounds like the *ch* in „teach". It does not — that is **ć**. **ź** is the soft, buzzing version. Trust the audio button, not that line of text.

### Pisanie

Write six sentences about yourself using the template from section 3. Then record yourself reading them out loud and send me the audio file.

The sentences must include:
- your name and where you are from,
- where you live and your age,
- one sentence with the word **lubię**.
